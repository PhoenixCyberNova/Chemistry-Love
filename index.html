Class 10 Chemistry Reference

Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations

1. Combustion of Magnesium Ribbon Combination
2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)
Dazzling white flame. Forms white powder of Magnesium Oxide.
2. Formation of Slaked Lime Exothermic Combination
CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat
Quicklime reacts vigorously with water producing slaked lime and heat.
3. Whitewashing Reaction (Action of CO₂) Combination
Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)
Forms a shiny white layer of Calcium Carbonate after 2-3 days.
4. Respiration Process Exothermic
C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) + 6O₂(aq) → 6CO₂(aq) + 6H₂O(l) + Energy
Glucose breaks down in cells to yield energy.
5. Thermal Decomposition of Ferrous Sulphate Thermal Decomposition
2FeSO₄(s) --Δ--> Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g)
Green FeSO₄·7H₂O crystals lose water, then decompose to reddish-brown Fe₂O₃ and suffocating sulphur gases.
6. Thermal Decomposition of Calcium Carbonate Thermal Decomposition
CaCO₃(s) --Δ--> CaO(s) + CO₂(g)
Used in manufacturing of cement.
7. Thermal Decomposition of Lead Nitrate Thermal Decomposition
2Pb(NO₃)₂(s) --Δ--> 2PbO(s) + 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g)
Produces brown fumes of Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂) and yellow Lead Oxide (PbO).
8. Electrolytic Decomposition of Water Electrolysis
2H₂O(l) --Electricity--> 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)
Volume of H₂ gas at cathode is double the volume of O₂ gas at anode.
9. Photolytic Decomposition of Silver Chloride Photolysis
2AgCl(s) --Sunlight--> 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g)
White AgCl turns grey in sunlight. Used in black and white photography.
10. Photolytic Decomposition of Silver Bromide Photolysis
2AgBr(s) --Sunlight--> 2Ag(s) + Br₂(g)
Pale yellow AgBr turns grey. Used in black and white photography.
11. Iron Nails in Copper Sulphate Displacement
Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)
Blue CuSO₄ turns light green; iron nail gets coated with reddish-brown copper deposit.
12. Zinc in Copper Sulphate Displacement
Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)
Blue solution becomes colourless.
13. Lead in Copper Chloride Displacement
Pb(s) + CuCl₂(aq) → PbCl₂(aq) + Cu(s)
Lead is more reactive than copper and displaces it.
14. Formation of Barium Sulphate Precipitate Double Displacement / Precipitation
Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)
Insoluble white precipitate of BaSO₄ is formed.
15. Lead Nitrate with Potassium Iodide Double Displacement / Precipitation
Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)
Forms a bright yellow precipitate of Lead Iodide.
16. Oxidation of Copper to Copper Oxide Oxidation
2Cu(s) + O₂(g) --Δ--> 2CuO(s)
Brown copper powder turns black due to the oxide layer.
17. Reduction of Copper Oxide Redox
CuO(s) + H₂(g) --Δ--> Cu(s) + H₂O(g)
Black CuO surface turns brown as copper is recovered.
18. Reduction of Zinc Oxide Redox
ZnO(s) + C(s) → Zn(s) + CO(g)
Carbon is oxidized to CO; ZnO is reduced to Zn.
19. Reaction of Manganese Dioxide with HCl Redox
MnO₂(s) + 4HCl(aq) → MnCl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) + Cl₂(g)
HCl is oxidized to Cl₂; MnO₂ is reduced to MnCl₂.

Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts

1. Acid + Metal Reaction Single Displacement
Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)
Hydrogen gas burns with a pop sound.
2. Base + Metal Reaction Special Base Reaction
2NaOH(aq) + Zn(s) → Na₂ZnO₂(aq) + H₂(g)
Forms Sodium Zincate and Hydrogen gas.
3. Metal Carbonate + Acid Gas Evolution
Na₂CO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)
Produces brisk effervescence due to CO₂ gas.
4. Metal Hydrogen Carbonate + Acid Gas Evolution
NaHCO₃(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)
Evolves CO₂ gas.
5. Lime Water Test for CO₂ Precipitation
Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)
Turns lime water milky due to insoluble Calcium Carbonate.
6. Excess CO₂ in Lime Water Solubility Reaction
CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) → Ca(HCO₃)₂(aq)
Milkiness disappears because Calcium Hydrogen Carbonate is soluble.
7. Neutralization Reaction Neutralization
NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)
Acid + Base → Salt + Water.
8. Metal Oxide + Acid Neutralization
CuO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CuCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
Black CuO dissolves; solution turns blue-green due to CuCl₂.
9. Non-Metal Oxide + Base Neutralization
Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)
Proves non-metallic oxides are acidic in nature.
10. Dissolution of Acid in Water Ionization
HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
Acids show acidic properties only in the presence of water.
11. Chlor-Alkali Process Electrolysis
2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) --Electricity--> 2NaOH(aq) + Cl₂(g) + H₂(g)
Cl₂ at anode, H₂ at cathode, NaOH near cathode.
12. Formation of Bleaching Powder Salt Preparation
Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O
Chlorine gas passed over dry slaked lime produces Bleaching Powder.
13. Thermal Decomposition of Baking Soda Thermal Decomposition
2NaHCO₃ --Δ--> Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂
Occurs while cooking; CO₂ released makes cakes soft and spongy.
14. Preparation of Washing Soda Crystallization
Na₂CO₃ + 10H₂O → Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
Recrystallization of anhydrous sodium carbonate.
15. Preparation of Plaster of Paris Dehydration
CaSO₄·2H₂O --373K--> CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O
Gypsum heated precisely at 373 K (100°C) forms POP.
16. Setting of Plaster of Paris Hydration
CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O
POP mixes with water to re-form hard solid Gypsum.

Chapter 3: Metals and Non-Metals

1. Combustion of Copper Oxidation
2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO
Forms black Copper(II) oxide.
2. Amphoteric Behavior of Aluminium Oxide (with Acid) Amphoteric Neutralization
Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O
Alumina acts as a base when reacting with acid.
3. Amphoteric Behavior of Aluminium Oxide (with Base) Amphoteric Neutralization
Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O
Alumina acts as an acid to form Sodium Aluminate.
4. Water Soluble Metal Oxides Base Formation
Na₂O(s) + H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq)
K₂O(s) + H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq)
Sodium and Potassium oxides dissolve in water to form alkalis.
5. Metal Reactions with Water / Steam Displacement
2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g) + Heat
Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)
2Al(s) + 3H₂O(g) → Al₂O₃(s) + 3H₂(g)
3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)
Na reacts violently with cold water; Ca floats; Al and Fe react only with steam.
6. Roasting of Sulphide Ores Roasting (Excess O₂)
2ZnS(s) + 3O₂(g) --Δ--> 2ZnO(s) + 2SO₂(g)
Heating sulphide ore in presence of excess air converts it to oxide.
7. Calcination of Carbonate Ores Calcination (Limited O₂)
ZnCO₃(s) --Δ--> ZnO(s) + CO₂(g)
Heating carbonate ore in limited air converts it to oxide.
8. Extraction of Low Reactivity Metals (Cinnabar) Thermal Reduction
2HgS(s) + 3O₂(g) --Δ--> 2HgO(s) + 2SO₂(g)
2HgO(s) --Δ--> 2Hg(l) + O₂(g)
Mercuric sulphide (Cinnabar) reduced to mercury by heating alone.
9. Thermite Reaction Exothermic Displacement
Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → 2Fe(l) + Al₂O₃(s) + Heat
Highly exothermic; molten iron generated is used to join railway tracks.

Chapter 4: Carbon and Its Compounds

1. Combustion of Carbon Compounds Combustion
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + Heat & Light
C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O + Heat & Light
Carbon compounds burn in oxygen releasing CO₂, water, heat, and light.
2. Oxidation of Ethanol to Ethanoic Acid Oxidation
CH₃CH₂OH --Alkaline KMnO₄ + Heat / Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ + Heat--> CH₃COOH
Alkaline KMnO₄ or Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ act as oxidizing agents.
3. Addition Reaction (Hydrogenation of Oils) Addition
R₂C=CR₂ + H₂ --Ni/Pd Catalyst--> R₂HC-CHR₂
Converts unsaturated vegetable oils to saturated fats using Nickel catalyst.
4. Substitution Reaction of Methane Substitution
CH₄ + Cl₂ --Sunlight--> CH₃Cl + HCl
Chlorine replaces hydrogen atoms one by one in the presence of sunlight.
5. Reaction of Ethanol with Sodium Metal Displacement
2Na + 2CH₃CH₂OH → 2CH₃CH₂O⁻Na⁺ + H₂
Produces Sodium Ethoxide and liberates Hydrogen gas with a pop.
6. Dehydration of Ethanol to Ethene Dehydration
CH₃CH₂OH --Hot Conc. H₂SO₄ (443 K)--> CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
Concentrated Sulphuric Acid acts as a dehydrating agent.
7. Esterification Reaction Esterification
CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH --Acid Catalyst--> CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O
Ethanoic acid + Ethanol yields sweet-smelling Ethyl Ethanoate (Ester).
8. Saponification (Soap Formation) Hydrolysis
CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH
Ester reacts with base to give alcohol and sodium salt of carboxylic acid (soap).
9. Reaction of Ethanoic Acid with Bases Neutralization
CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O
Forms Sodium Acetate (Sodium Ethanoate) and Water.
10. Reaction of Ethanoic Acid with Carbonates & Hydrogen Carbonates Gas Evolution
2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂
CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂
Gives brisk effervescence due to CO₂ gas.
